## Overview

The **Sringeri Sharada Peetham** (*Śṛṅgeri Śāradā Pīṭham*; शृङ्गेरी शारदा पीठम्), formally the Dakshinamnaya Sri Sharada Peetham, stands at Sringeri on the banks of the river Tuṅgā, in the Chikkamagaluru district of Karnataka.[^1][^2] It is a seat of the [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita Vedānta]] tradition. On its own account, it is the first of the four *āmnāya* pīṭhas that [[adi-shankaracharya|Ādi Śaṅkarācārya]] established more than twelve centuries ago.[^1]

## Significance

According to the Pīṭham, Śaṅkara established four centres in the four corners of the country to safeguard and spread the four Vedas, and placed his disciples at their head.[^1] The ācāryas of Śṛṅgeri are styled Jagadguru Śaṅkarācārya.[^1] Scholarship records more cautiously that Śaṅkara *purportedly* established the earliest monastic system in Brahmanical religion, including the Daśanāmī renouncer orders and four or five principal monasteries across the subcontinent.[^3]

## History

The Pīṭham's tradition tells how Śaṅkara chose the site after seeing a cobra sheltering a frog on the banks of the Tuṅgā.[^1] He appointed his foremost disciple, [[sureshvara|Sureśvara]], as its first ācārya.[^1] According to the Pīṭham, its twelfth ācārya, Vidyāraṇya, guided and inspired the founding of the Vijayanagara empire in the 14th century.[^1] Vidyāraṇya is also counted among the later philosophers of Advaita.[^4]

## Associated traditions and figures

The Pīṭham belongs to the Advaita Vedānta tradition of Śaṅkara and his disciples, among whom Sureśvara wrote the *Naiṣkarmyasiddhi*, an independent treatise on Advaita.[^4] Its line of ācāryas continues to the present.[^1]
