## Overview

**Vyasa** (*Vyāsa*; व्यास) is the sage whom the [[mahabharata|Mahābhārata]] names as its composer: the epic presents itself as the work of Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana.[^1] It explains his name, which means "arranger" or "compiler", by his arranging of the Vedas.[^1] He also appears as a character in the story he tells, and as grandfather of the Pāṇḍavas.[^1] Several later works are ascribed to "Vyāsa", among them the oldest commentary on the Yoga Sūtras.[^2]

## Life

### Traditional account

According to the Mahābhārata, Vyāsa was born to Satyavatī through the sage Parāśara, on an island in the river Yamunā. Because he was born on an island he was called Dvaipāyana, "island-born".[^1] Satyavatī later recalls that he was also called Kṛṣṇa, "the dark", for his dark colour.[^1] As soon as he was born he set his mind on asceticism and left, promising his mother that he would come whenever she called him.[^1]

Seeing that righteousness declines with each age (*yuga*), and with it the span and strength of human life, he arranged the Vedas, and for this he came to be called Vyāsa. The epic also says that he divided the Vedas into four parts.[^1] Later, at his mother's request, he fathered the heirs of the Kuru line, Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Pāṇḍu, and also Vidura.[^1] The epic sets these events in the generation before the war between the Kauravas and the Pāṇḍavas, which it places in the interval between the Dvāpara and Kali yugas.[^1]

### Historical perspective

Modern scholarship treats ascriptions to Vyāsa cautiously. In discussing the oldest commentary on the Yoga Sūtras, which tradition ascribes to Vyāsa, Edwin Bryant refers to its author as "the legendary Vyāsa" and dates the commentary to around the 4th–5th century CE.[^2]

## Teachings

The Mahābhārata presents Vyāsa above all as a transmitter of sacred knowledge. He taught the Vedas, with the Mahābhārata as a fifth, to his disciples Sumanta, Jaimini, Paila, his son Śuka and Vaiśampāyana, and published the epic through them.[^1] The [[bhagavad-gita|Bhagavad Gītā]], Kṛṣṇa's teaching to Arjuna at the start of the epic's sixth book, comes down as part of the work ascribed to him.[^3]

## Works

The Mahābhārata, including the Bhagavad Gītā, is ascribed to Vyāsa within the epic itself.[^1] The arrangement of the Vedas into four is likewise credited to him there.[^1] The [[yoga-bhashya|Yoga Bhāṣya]], the oldest commentary on the Yoga Sūtras, is ascribed to "Vyāsa", though Bryant dates it to around the 4th–5th century CE.[^2]

## Lineage

In the Mahābhārata's account Vyāsa is the son of Parāśara and the teacher of Sumanta, Jaimini, Paila, Śuka and Vaiśampāyana. Vaiśampāyana recites the epic at King Janamejaya's snake sacrifice.[^1]
