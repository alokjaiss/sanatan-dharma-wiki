## Overview

**Patanjali** (*Patañjali*; पतञ्जलि) is the author of the [[yoga-sutra|Yoga Sūtras]], 195 aphorisms in four chapters that are the foundational text of the [[yoga-darshana|Yoga]] darśana.[^1][^3] He is not the founder of yoga, whose origins long preceded him.[^1]

## Life

### Traditional account

Tradition, first evidenced in the commentary of King Bhoja in the 11th century CE, holds that Patañjali was the same Patañjali who wrote the primary commentary on Pāṇini's grammar, and also ascribes to him a treatise on medicine.[^1]

### Historical perspective

The date of his text cannot be fixed precisely. Most scholars date the Yoga Sūtras shortly after the turn of the common era, around the 1st–2nd century CE, though some place them several centuries earlier; they are no later than the 5th century CE.[^1]

## Teachings

Patañjali defines yoga as *citta-vṛtti-nirodha*, the stilling of all the states of the mind (1.2).[^1] His framework is the dualism shared with [[samkhya|Sāṅkhya]], of conscious selves (*puruṣa*) and primordial matter (*prakṛti*). Where Sāṅkhya pursues the self's liberation mainly by reasoning, Yoga pursues it by meditation, concentrating on the nature of mind and consciousness.[^1] He sets out an eightfold path of practice, from ethical abstentions and observances through posture and breath control to concentration, meditation and absorption (*samādhi*). He also allows that the goal may be reached through devotion to the Lord, Īśvara.[^1][^3]

## Works

Patañjali is known for the Yoga Sūtras. The traditional identification with the grammarian of the same name would add the great commentary on Pāṇini, and tradition also ascribes a medical treatise to him.[^1]

## Legacy and institutions

Patañjali's sūtras were read through the commentary ascribed to [[vyasa|Vyāsa]], the [[yoga-bhashya|Yoga Bhāṣya]]. As Bryant puts it, the philosophy of Patañjali is in practice Patañjali as Vyāsa understood him, since every later commentator built on Vyāsa.[^1] Yoga is counted among the six āstika darśanas of Hindu philosophy.[^2]
