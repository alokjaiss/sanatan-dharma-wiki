## Definition

**Dharma** (*dharma*; धर्म) is a term with a wide range of meanings. The Monier-Williams dictionary begins its entry with "that which is established or firm, steadfast decree, statute, ordinance, law". It goes on to list "usage, practice, customary observance or prescribed conduct, duty", "right, justice", and "virtue, morality, religion, religious merit, good works".[^1]

## Etymology

The Monier-Williams dictionary derives *dharma* from the verbal root *dhṛ*, and its first sense is "that which is established or firm".[^1]

## In the scriptures

The [[mimamsa|Mīmāṃsā]] school, which is devoted to interpreting the Vedas, opens its root text with an enquiry into dharma and immediately defines it:

> *athāto dharmajijñāsā*\
> *codanālakṣaṇo ’rtho dharmaḥ*
>
> "Now, therefore, the enquiry into dharma. Dharma is a good (*artha*) marked by injunction (*codanā*)." (translation: Sanatan Dharma Wiki)
>
> — Jaimini, Mīmāṃsā Sūtra 1.1.1–2[^3]

The [[bhagavad-gita|Bhagavad Gītā]] teaches that it is better to perform one's own duty (*svadharma*) imperfectly than another's duty well (3.35). Later in the text it connects a person's duty with their nature (18.41).[^4]

## Interpretations across traditions

For the Mīmāṃsā school, dharma is an injunction (*codanā*) that is marked by bringing about welfare (*artha*), as the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy renders the definition.[^2] On this view dharma is known through the Vedas, which Mīmāṃsā holds to be an eternal, unauthored corpus.[^2] The Gītā's teaching of *svadharma* ties dharma to each person's own duties.[^4] Some readings, like the one the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy gives, take this specifically as caste duty.[^4]
