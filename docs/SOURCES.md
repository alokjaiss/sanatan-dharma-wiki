# Sources

Where entries may draw their facts and quotations from, by kind. The encyclopedia cites only sources that were actually consulted, and records the terms under which original-language texts are reproduced.

## Primary texts

Original-language texts and their locators.

| Source | Notes |
|---|---|
| [GRETIL](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/) (Göttingen Register of Electronic Texts in Indian Languages) | Romanised e-texts of most major works. Files are marked "for reference purposes only": quote short passages with attribution, and link rather than mirror. In `readOnline`, use `license: link-only`. |
| [Sanskrit Wikisource](https://sa.wikisource.org/) | Devanagari texts, often with traditional commentaries. Check each page's source edition. |

## Public-domain translations

May be quoted, with translator and year:

- R. T. H. Griffith, *The Hymns of the Rigveda* (2nd ed., 1896–97)
- K. T. Telang, *The Bhagavadgîtâ* (Sacred Books of the East 8, 1882)
- G. Thibaut, *The Vedânta-Sûtras with the Commentary by Śaṅkarâkârya* (SBE 34, 1890; SBE 38, 1896)
- J. H. Woods, *The Yoga-System of Patañjali* (Harvard Oriental Series 17, 1914)

- F. Max Müller, *The Upanishads* (SBE 1, 1879; SBE 15, 1884)

## Scholarship

For history, dating and analysis:

- [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/) and [Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/), signed peer-reviewed articles. Cite the author.
- University-press books and peer-reviewed journals, for example the *Electronic Journal of Vedic Studies*.
- *Brill's Encyclopedia of Hinduism*.

## Reference works

For orientation and basic facts:

- [Monier-Williams Sanskrit-English Dictionary](https://www.sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/), via the Cologne Digital Sanskrit Dictionaries, for etymology and senses
- [UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/) inscription records (Memory of the World, Intangible Cultural Heritage)
- Encyclopaedia Britannica and Oxford Reference, when they can be consulted

## Traditional sources

A tradition's own account of itself. Always cite it as such (`kind: traditional`), alongside scholarship where the two differ:

- Publications and official sites of maṭhas, pīṭhas and sampradāyas, for example [Sringeri Sharada Peetham](https://www.sringeri.net/) and [Kanchi Kamakoti Peetham](https://www.kamakoti.org/)
- Traditional histories and hagiographies (Śaṅkaradigvijaya, guruparamparā texts), cited as what they say rather than as historical fact

## Not acceptable as the only source

Wikipedia (use it to find sources, then cite those), blogs, forums, videos, quotation sites, AI-generated sites and print-on-demand compilations.
